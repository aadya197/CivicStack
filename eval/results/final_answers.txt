There seems to be a misunderstanding in your question.

**A Private Limited company in India is NOT allowed to have unlimited public shareholders.**

In fact, the very definition and characteristics of a Private Limited company under the Companies Act, 2013 (Section 2(68)) impose strict limitations that prevent it from having "unlimited public shareholders":

1.  **Maximum Shareholder Limit:** A Private Limited company is restricted to a maximum of **200 members (shareholders)**. This limit explicitly prevents it from having an "unlimited" number of shareholders.
2.  **Prohibition on Public Invitation:** A Private Limited company is **prohibited from inviting the public to subscribe for any shares or debentures** of the company. This means it cannot issue shares through an Initial Public Offering (IPO) or any other public solicitation. Its capital is raised privately from a limited group of individuals or entities.
3.  **Restriction on Transferability of Shares:** The Articles of Association of a Private Limited company restrict the right to transfer its shares. Shares cannot be freely traded on a stock exchange like those of a public company.

**In contrast, it is a Public Limited company that:**

*   Can have an unlimited number of shareholders (minimum of 7, no upper limit).
*   Can invite the public to subscribe for its shares and debentures (e.g., through an IPO).
*   Allows for the free transferability of its shares, which can be listed and traded on stock exchanges.

The distinction between a Private Limited company and a Public Limited company is fundamental in Indian company law, primarily revolving around the ability to raise capital from the public and the number of shareholders.

In India, a company name reserved through the SPICe+ Part A form (or earlier, the RUN form) is initially valid for **20 days** from the date of approval.

You can extend this reserved name for additional periods, provided you apply *before* the current reservation period expires. The extension options and associated fees are as follows:

1.  **Extension for 40 days:**
    *   **Total validity:** 60 days from the date of approval (initial 20 + 40).
    *   **Fee:** INR 1,000

2.  **Extension for 60 days:**
    *   **Total validity:** 80 days from the date of approval (initial 20 + 60).
    *   **Fee:** INR 2,000

3.  **Extension for 120 days:**
    *   **Total validity:** 140 days from the date of approval (initial 20 + 120).
    *   **Fee:** INR 3,000

**Important Points:**

*   The application for extension must be made through the MCA (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) portal *before* the existing reservation period (initial 20 days or any subsequent extended period) lapses.
*   If the name reservation expires, the name becomes available for others to reserve, and you would have to apply for a fresh name reservation.
*   These fees are subject to change by the MCA, so it's always advisable to check the latest notifications on the official MCA website.
